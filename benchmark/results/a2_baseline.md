# A2 — external discrimination backtest: the V1 baseline

**Basis.** V1 at commit `901ce53`, engine and config untouched. 18 admitted failure
cases against the 43 adopted companies of D65 as the survivor panel, each assessed at
the *same* point-in-time cutoff through the same `assess()` call. Flag rule `grade >= 5`,
frozen before this run (D82). Generated by `benchmark/harness/run_a2.py` and scored by
`benchmark/harness/score_a2.py`; the raw record is `a2_raw.json`.

**Read the limits section before quoting any figure here.**

**AUC was computed two ways and the two agree to machine precision** — a pairwise count
and the Mann-Whitney rank identity. The check was not decorative: the rank version first

an inverted orientation. One implementation would have published either number with
nothing to contradict it.

median of their own survivor panel.** That claim needs no flag rule and no band edge.

**A 13-point separation on escalation is close to no separation at all.** D72b parked the
escalation threshold on the grounds that firing in 31% of periods "feels high" with no
evidence base for moving it. There is now an evidence base, and it is this row. High
severity separates better at 27 points but still flags 45% of healthy company-cutoffs.
Both were rejected as flag rules on this evidence (D82).

## The coverage failure, reported rather than hidden

| ticker | event | categories scored | grade shown | uncapped | score |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| HTZ | Chapter 11, 2020-05-22 | **1/5** | **4** | 2 | 80.0 |

Hertz is excluded from every metric above because the engine cannot see it: `total_debt`
refuses with `NO_DEBT_DATA`, and `ebit`, `cash`, `current_assets` and
`current_liabilities` all fail to resolve. Fleet debt sits in dimensioned securitisation
contexts the companyfacts endpoint does not return, and Hertz files an unclassified
balance sheet. **This is D25's Ford finding recurring on a different company.**

The consequence is worse than an absence. Hertz's grade 4 rests on a single computed
number — revenue grew 8% — giving business performance 8/10, rescaled to 80.0, uncapped
grade 2, capped to 4. So sixteen months before Chapter 11 the engine rates Hertz **better
than every failure it could actually see**, all of which are grade 5 or 6. The graduated
cap is a ceiling, never a floor: it cannot express "we know nothing", and the least
knowable company in the cohort receives a mid-scale grade. Counting this as a miss would
blame the discrimination model for a coverage defect; hiding it would overstate coverage.
It is reported as its own row and carried into A3 as the witness for missing information
that should reduce confidence.

## What this does NOT establish

**Discrimination, on a small sample. Not calibration.** Nothing here maps a grade to a
default probability, a spread or a loss rate, and 18 events could not support such a map
even in principle. The grades remain an internal analytical scale.

### Why the survivor denominator is 774 and not 817

Both numbers are correct and they count different things. Stated explicitly because a
denominator that changes between reports without explanation undermines everything
reported beside it.

| | arithmetic | what it counts |
|---|---|---|
| **817** | 19 cutoffs × 43 survivors | every survivor at every cutoff of all **19 verified** cases |
| **774** | 18 cutoffs × 43 survivors | every survivor at every cutoff of the **18 admitted** cases |

Nineteen cases were verified and each has a distinct point-in-time cutoff, so nineteen
survivor panels exist in `a2_raw.json`, each holding all 43 companies. **Hertz is then
excluded from the discrimination set** as a coverage failure — 1 of 5 categories scored —
and its cutoff of 2019-05-23 is shared with no other case, so removing Hertz removes
exactly one panel of 43. **817 − 43 = 774.**

Every discrimination metric in this file uses **774**, because a false-positive rate must
be computed over the same cutoffs as the sensitivity standing beside it. 817 belongs only
to statements about all nineteen panels. The escalation and High-severity reach figures
below are the 774-based ones; measured over all 817 the escalated reach is also 37%, so no
conclusion here turns on the choice — but that is a coincidence of this dataset, not a
reason to leave it unsaid.

**The false-positive rate is pseudo-replicated, and the honest number is the worse one.**
774 "survivor company-cutoffs" are 43 distinct companies observed at up to 18 cutoffs
each — **not 774 independent observations**. A company weak at one cutoff is usually weak
at the next, so the pooled 21% has far less precision than its denominator implies. The
distinct-company figure is the one to quote when asking what an analyst's desk would
look like: **21 of 43 healthy companies — 49% — are flagged at least once.** Those two
numbers describe the same rule and read completely differently.

The flagged survivors are also not random. They are dominated by travel, leisure and
gaming — CCL, RCL, MAR, MGM, LVS, WYNN, PENN, CZR, HLT — flagged largely at the
COVID-era cutoffs, plus leveraged media and telecom. A cruise operator in 2020 *did* look
like a default candidate on its financials. That makes these honest false positives
rather than arbitrary ones, and it also means the rate is period-dependent.

**The dev and held-out distinct-company shares are not comparable to each other.** dev
covers 10 cutoffs and held-out 8, and the share rises mechanically with the number of
cutoffs a company could have been flagged at. Compare each split against itself across
versions, never across splits.

**The cohort cannot reach the 2008-09 credit cycle.** Measured: Charter's companyfacts
begins 2011-05-03, so a 2008 cutoff yields zero facts. XBRL did not exist. Every event
here therefore falls in 2019-2023, and **11 of 18 are 2020** — one macro shock, COVID
plus the oil-price collapse. The cohort is 8 retail/consumer, 5 energy, 3
transport/industrial, 2 telecom, which reflects which sectors actually defaulted rather
than any sampling choice.

**It does not test whether the engine predicted the shock**, and must not be read as
doing so. A 2018 balance sheet cannot contain COVID. What it tests is whether the
pre-event financials showed *vulnerability* — leverage, coverage and cash generation
already weak enough to not survive a shock. That is the question a credit analyst
actually asks, but it is a narrower question than "did it predict the default".

**Lead times vary, 421 to 786 days** (median 547), because fiscal calendars and filing
dates do not line up with petition dates. Every case clears the 12-month minimum and
several exceed two years. Longer lead makes the test harder, not easier, so this biases
against the engine — but it means "12-month horizon" would be the wrong description of
what was measured.

**Survivorship is defined relative to the cutoff, not absolutely.** CHTR filed Item 1.03
8-Ks in 2009; it is a valid survivor at every cutoff here and the harness enforces that
with an `excluded_before` field rather than trusting a comment. Item 2.04 filings
(obligation acceleration) exist for six survivors and were deliberately **not** treated
as defaults — reading them as such without checking each document would manufacture
defaults out of routine refinancing.

**One number in this file came from a judgment, not a measurement:** the admission bar of
3 of 5 categories. It is defensible and it is stated, but a different bar would move
which cases are in the cohort. HTZ is the only case it currently excludes.
